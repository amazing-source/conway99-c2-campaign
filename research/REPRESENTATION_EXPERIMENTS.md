# Representation experiments

This is a chronological conceptual ledger, not a claim of priority.

## 1. Completion viewpoint

Starting primitive: complete signed core `N`, unknown matching `D`.

Outcome: T4 proves real completion exactness and reconstruction.

## 2. Ternary/capacity viewpoint

Starting primitive: rank-15 labelled ternary projector plus pointwise capacities.

Outcome: exact lift to the signed integer equations, but no global contradiction.

## 3. Coupled `(Q,N)` viewpoint

Theorem C eliminates `D` as an independent variable.

Each pair becomes one of four states:

\[
(0,0),(2,0),(1,-1),(1,+1).
\]

This became the substrate for the native-system research.

## 4. Object Round 1

Five mixed objects were screened.

- signed 4-cycle split: reduced to slack mixed-moment identities;
- `D` pinching: reverted to known pair equations / interlacing;
- Gaussian-unit matrix `Z`: low-degree universal spectral identity falsified by BvLS;
- mod-12/idempotent packaging: repackaging of known mod-2/mod-3 projectors;
- `D`-rectangle / block-weight structure: compression plus entrywise exact equations; no new signed law.

Outcome: no surviving object. Theorem C itself remained useful as a structural interface.

## 5. Pair-table information-loss analysis

A 16-cell joint transition table was projected separately into `Q`-visible and `N`-visible data.

The projection has a two-dimensional kernel. A hidden signed coordinate can vary while the separate projections agree.

Gluing those pair tables produced one genuine joint local rule, but it was a rediscovery of the known star/antistar structure.

Outcome: joint coupling genuinely contains information, but the first extracted information was already known.

## 6. Bridge graphs

For every exterior involution-orbit, the two lifted neighborhoods define a bridge graph: two paths plus cycles, with phase/pairing data.

Novel local language, but all minimal overlaps realize every pairing combination. A stronger law would require whole paths / much larger neighborhoods.

Outcome: route closed before local-radius escalation.

## 7. Port graphs / link involutions / debt ledger

A representation-mutation run deliberately abandoned pair-state primitives.

### A · port graphs

Whole rows become signed multigraphs on the seven coordinates.

Result: exact row laws and very large row counts; isolated rows are highly flexible.

### B · coordinate link pairings

Coordinate-local pairings encode much of the coordinate-sharing layer.

Determinacy test: even with `D` and 14 pairings fixed, the disjoint-cell layer is not determined. An exact two-row test reported 1,257 realizations and identified alternating closed-trail sign flips as the minimal sign ambiguity.

Refining the representation requires the unsigned disjoint layer plus trail phase bits, effectively recreating the original state.

Outcome: no compression; route closed.

### C · debt / receipt ledger

Raw extension-order arithmetic is symmetric and associative. No naive order defect exists.

Outcome: useful negative result.

## 8. Two-faced-board debt

A blind native-system session independently rediscovered the 21 `D`-pair block weights

\[
E_{AB}\in\{0,1,2\}
\]

and the centered debt

\[
\zeta_{AB}=E_{AB}-1.
\]

The row conservation

\[
\sum_B\zeta_{AB}=0
\]

is the already-known `#w0=#w2` balance.

Outcome: structurally natural representation, but duplicate/repackaging.

## 9. Response-card boundary

Attempt: quotient remaining labels by identical future one-step effects.

Exact derivation shows a response-card type contains the root label plus the complete relation history to the completed rows.

Because the actual 42 positive D7 roots are distinct, the concrete C2 quotient is singleton immediately.

Outcome: exact but non-compressive.

## Meta-conclusion

The campaign has repeatedly found **high local extendability** in unrelated representations.

This does not decide EX/NOT-EX.

It does justify a hard methodological rule:

> Do not respond to failure by merely increasing local radius.

The remaining conceptual target is a global compatibility language or a constructive extension grammar.
